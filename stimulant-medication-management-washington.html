ADHD Stimulant Medication Management in Washington State

Mind & Metabolism · Elizabeth Calfos, DNP, PMHNP-BC · Reviewed October 2026

Book Now Contact Us Call (253) 525-5536

If you have ADHD and need a prescriber for Adderall, Vyvanse, Ritalin, or another stimulant medication, Mind & Metabolism can help. We're a psychiatric practice in Lakewood, Washington offering telehealth to adults and adolescents (13+) across the state. Our board-certified psychiatric providers, including Elizabeth Calfos, DNP, PMHNP-BC, manage stimulant prescriptions as part of ongoing ADHD care.

Stimulants are the most commonly prescribed medication for ADHD, and for many people they make a meaningful difference in focus, follow-through, and daily function. But they're also Schedule II controlled substances, which means they come with specific legal requirements around prescribing and monitoring. We handle all of that clearly and without unnecessary hassle.

What we prescribe

We prescribe the full range of stimulant and non-stimulant ADHD medications, depending on what's right for you.

Adderall / Adderall XR Mixed amphetamine salts, immediate and extended release
Vyvanse Lisdexamfetamine, long-acting amphetamine prodrug
Ritalin / Concerta Methylphenidate, immediate and extended release
Focalin / Focalin XR Dexmethylphenidate, immediate and extended release
Strattera Atomoxetine, non-stimulant option
Wellbutrin / Qelbree Non-stimulant alternatives when stimulants aren't the right fit

How stimulant prescribing works

Before prescribing any stimulant, we complete a full psychiatric evaluation. This applies whether you're new to ADHD treatment or transferring from another provider. We review your history, confirm the diagnosis, and make sure there are no medical or psychiatric reasons a stimulant would be risky for you.

Once treatment starts, you'll have regular follow-up appointments. Federal law doesn't allow refills on Schedule II medications, so each prescription is issued new by your provider. We make this as easy as possible with brief telehealth check-ins. Most refill visits take 10 to 15 minutes.

Under temporary federal rules that run through December 31, 2026, stimulants can be prescribed by telehealth without an in-person visit. If those rules change, we'll tell you what's required and help you meet it.

Who we see

We see adults and adolescents ages 13 and older. You must be physically located in Washington State at the time of your appointment. We accept most major insurance plans, including Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, Premera, Regence, Medicare, Molina, and others. See our full insurance list.

If you're transferring from another prescriber, like a primary care doctor, a previous psychiatrist, or a telehealth-only service that's closing, we can take over your care. We'll review your records and medication history as part of the intake process.

What we don't do

We're an outpatient psychiatric practice, and we don't offer quick prescriptions. Every patient goes through a proper evaluation, and medication decisions are based on clinical judgment. If stimulants aren't appropriate for you, we'll tell you why and discuss alternatives. We also don't prescribe stimulants to patients who are in active substance use that makes them unsafe. That's clinical best practice and protects your safety.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get Adderall prescribed by telehealth in Washington State?

Yes, for now. Temporary federal rules that run through December 31, 2026 allow stimulants to be prescribed by telehealth after a full psychiatric evaluation, without an in-person visit. If those rules change, we'll explain what's required.

Do I need a new evaluation if I was already diagnosed with ADHD?

If you have a prior ADHD diagnosis, we still complete an intake evaluation to review your history and confirm the diagnosis before prescribing. It's good clinical practice and expected for any controlled substance prescription.

What stimulant medications do you prescribe?

We prescribe stimulant medications used for ADHD including Adderall, Adderall XR, Vyvanse, Ritalin, Concerta, and Focalin, as well as non-stimulant alternatives like Strattera and Wellbutrin. The right medication depends on your history, health, and what has or hasn't worked before.

How often do I need follow-up appointments?

Stimulants are Schedule II controlled substances, and federal law doesn't allow refills. Follow-ups are usually monthly at first and set by your provider based on how you're doing. Most are brief telehealth visits.

Does insurance cover these appointments?

Most major plans cover outpatient psychiatric visits for ADHD management. The medication itself is covered separately under your pharmacy benefit. See our accepted insurance.
