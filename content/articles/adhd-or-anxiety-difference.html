"I can't focus" is one of the most common things we hear in a first visit. Sometimes that's ADHD. Sometimes it's anxiety. Often it's some of both. The two can look surprisingly similar from the outside, and even from the inside, which is why getting the answer right matters so much.

Where they overlap

Both ADHD and anxiety can cause trouble concentrating, restlessness, irritability, poor sleep, and that feeling of being behind on everything. Both can make you procrastinate. Both can leave you exhausted by the end of the day. If you only look at the surface, they're easy to confuse.

Where they differ

  • Why your mind wanders. With anxiety, attention gets pulled toward worries: what could go wrong, what someone thought of you. With ADHD, attention drifts toward whatever is more interesting, or bounces around without a clear reason.
  • When it started. ADHD symptoms begin in childhood, even if nobody called it ADHD at the time. Anxiety can begin at any age and often has a clearer starting point or trigger.
  • What happens when the pressure is off. Anxiety often eases on a relaxed weekend. ADHD tends to stick around even when life is calm.
  • The role of interest. People with ADHD can often focus intensely on things they enjoy. Anxiety usually doesn't make that exception.

When it's both

Many adults have both. Years of missed deadlines and forgotten details can create real anxiety on top of ADHD. In that case, treating only the anxiety leaves the root problem in place, and treating only the ADHD may not settle the worry. A good plan addresses both, usually starting with whichever is causing the most trouble.

Why the right diagnosis matters

Some medications that help anxiety don't help ADHD, and some ADHD medications can make anxiety worse at first if the dose isn't right. That's why we take time during a psychiatric evaluation to sort out what's really going on before recommending anything.
