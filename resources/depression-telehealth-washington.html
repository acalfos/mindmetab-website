Getting Depression Treatment via Telehealth in Washington State

By the clinical team at Mind & Metabolism · Updated June 2026 · 5 min read

Depression is more than feeling sad. It's a weight that affects how you think, sleep, eat, work, and connect with the people around you. And for many people in Washington State, getting help has historically meant long waits, long drives, and navigating a system that isn't built for how people actually live.

Telehealth has changed that. Today, you can connect with a board-certified psychiatric provider from your home, get an accurate diagnosis, and start a treatment plan without ever sitting in a waiting room.

Is telehealth effective for depression?

Yes. Multiple studies have found that telehealth psychiatric care produces outcomes comparable to in-person care for depression. The quality of the evaluation, the relationship with your provider, and the treatment itself are what matter, not the physical location of the appointment.

In Washington State, telehealth mental health services are covered by most major insurance plans under the state's telehealth parity laws, meaning your insurer must cover telehealth visits the same way they cover in-person visits.

What makes depression different from normal sadness?

Depression is a clinical condition, not a phase or a personality trait. Signs that you may be experiencing depression include:

  • Persistent low mood lasting more than two weeks
  • Loss of interest in things you used to enjoy
  • Changes in sleep, like sleeping too much or too little
  • Changes in appetite or weight
  • Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
  • Fatigue that doesn't improve with rest
  • Feelings of worthlessness or guilt
  • Thoughts of death or self-harm

If you're experiencing several of these, speaking with a provider is the right next step. Depression is highly treatable, but it rarely improves on its own without support.

If you are having thoughts of suicide or self-harm, please call or text 988 to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, available 24 hours a day.

What does a telehealth depression evaluation look like?

Your first appointment is a conversation, not a checklist. A good psychiatric provider will ask about your symptoms, how long you've had them, your personal and family history, your sleep patterns, your physical health, and what's been happening in your life. This takes time, and it should.

At the end of your first visit, you'll have a clear diagnosis, a treatment plan, and a follow-up appointment already scheduled. You leave with next steps in hand.

What are the treatment options for depression?

Depression treatment is not one-size-fits-all. Options include:

  • Antidepressant medication. SSRIs and SNRIs are the most commonly prescribed and well-studied medications for depression. Finding the right medication and dose can take a few weeks, and your provider will monitor your response closely.
  • Therapy with a therapist. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) and other evidence-based approaches can be highly effective, especially in combination with medication for moderate-to-severe depression.
  • Addressing metabolic factors. Thyroid dysfunction, vitamin D deficiency, sleep apnea, and other physical conditions can either cause or worsen depression. A thorough evaluation looks at these too.

Does telehealth work for severe depression?

Telehealth is appropriate for mild to moderate depression and for many patients with severe depression who are stable and not in crisis. If you are in crisis or your symptoms are severe enough to require a higher level of care, your provider will help coordinate the right resources. Telehealth and outpatient care are not substitutes for emergency or inpatient care when that level of support is needed.

How to get started with telehealth depression care in Washington State

  1. Choose a board-certified psychiatric provider who accepts your insurance
  2. Request an appointment online or by phone
  3. Complete a short intake form before your first visit
  4. Attend your first telehealth appointment from wherever you are
  5. Walk away with a diagnosis and a plan

At Mind & Metabolism, we see patients across Washington State via secure telehealth. Most major insurance is accepted. New patients can request care through our online portal and are typically seen within a few days.
